The AI Safety Network

Hello! I built this resource as part of a hackathon. It tells the story of the AI-safety field and shows the connections running through it. It began during my job search: reading about orgs and new research, I kept seeing the same names come up — so this project tries to fill the gaps in how that story usually gets told, and to serve as an entry point into the field.
Disclaimer

The opinions and takes here are entirely my own. The research was done with the assistance of Claude, many conversations, and a lot of online reading. It's a work in progress — one day I'd love to scrape X for all the hot goss. This was built during a hackathon hosted by BlueDot, after living as a pet project of mine for quite a while.

1

How it all began

1990s
a mailing list argues about the far future
The 1990s

Before the field had a name, its ideas circulated on the extropians mailing list — a transhumanist scene where a teenage Eliezer Yudkowsky, Nick Bostrom, Anders Sandberg, and the future founders of DeepMind all argued about the far future.

yudkowsky> accelerating a benevolent superintelligence is humanity's most urgent project.
bostrom> extinction risk isn't science fiction — it's formal probability.
sandberg> so what, exactly, do we build first?
illustrative paraphrase of the scene — explore the real archive below
👉 Explore the Extropians Archive Index · interactive version on LessWrong
1998

Bostrom and philosopher David Pearce found the World Transhumanist Association — the mailing-list scene gets its first institution. The WTA is a membership nonprofit advocating the ethical use of technology to overcome human limits: aging, disease, cognitive constraints. Its ideology is techno-optimist humanism — the future should be radically better, on purpose. Rebranded as Humanity+ in 2008 and based in the US, it still exists today, running conferences and publications. Its role in this story is simple: it gave the scene its first real institution — and gave Bostrom practice building them.

2000

Eliezer Yudkowsky co-founds the Singularity Institute for Artificial Intelligence (SIAI) alongside Brian and Sabine Atkins. At inception, the mission is the exact opposite of modern AI safety: its explicit goal is to accelerate the development of artificial intelligence.

2

Shouting into the void

2000–2014
big ideas, tiny audience — and the first real money
2005

Realizing that an unconstrained intelligent agent will naturally optimize away human utility, Yudkowsky pivots SIAI away from acceleration and toward catastrophic-risk mitigation. Simultaneously, across the Atlantic, Nick Bostrom founds the Future of Humanity Institute (FHI) at Oxford, legitimizing existential risk within elite global academia.

2005–06

Venture capitalist Peter Thiel begins funding SIAI with a $200,000 matching grant and steps in as an official advisor. This capital establishes the annual Singularity Summit.

2006–09

The Rationalist Bible. To build a recruiting pipeline of clear thinkers capable of solving alignment, Yudkowsky writes a massive daily series of essays on Overcoming Bias, which spins off to form the community site LessWrong in 2009. It becomes the field's main recruiting funnel for a decade.

SCENE
2008 — The FOOM debate

The field's first schism. Yudkowsky and economist Robin Hanson engage in a legendary, months-long written debate over fast vs. slow takeoff (FOOM).

Yudkowsky
"Once an AI can improve itself, it goes FOOM — from village idiot to beyond Einstein before anyone can react."
Hanson
"No single lab explodes. Growth arrives gradually, spread across the whole economy — like every revolution before it."
paraphrased from the written debate — read the real exchange (collected by MIRI)
2010

Demis Hassabis, Shane Legg, and Mustafa Suleyman found DeepMind in London. The critical initial funding check comes directly from Peter Thiel — secured because of a personal introduction Yudkowsky made at the Singularity Summit.

The fanfic recruiter: simultaneously, Yudkowsky begins publishing Harry Potter and the Methods of Rationality, a fanfiction phenomenon that becomes the field's single most effective cultural recruiting tool for young software engineers.

2009–11 — The EA institutions

In Oxford, philosophers Toby Ord and Will MacAskill found Giving What We Can (2009), a pledge to give 10% of your income to the most effective charities. Two years later, MacAskill and Benjamin Todd found 80,000 Hours, which applies the same logic to careers. Together these become the institutional core of effective altruism.

80,000 Hours later ranks AI safety among its top recommended career paths, making it one of the field's biggest talent funnels. Ord, also an FHI researcher, becomes one of existential risk's most credible mainstream voices.

2011

Cari Tuna, a former Wall Street Journal reporter, and Facebook co-founder Dustin Moskovitz met on a blind date in 2009 and later married. In 2011 they establish Good Ventures, pledging to give away nearly all of their fortune along effective-altruist lines, working in lockstep with GiveWell Labs, the precursor to Open Philanthropy. AI risk becomes one of its top cause areas, and most of the money in the rest of this story arrives through this lane.

2012–13

In 2012, Jaan Tallinn co-founds the Centre for the Study of Existential Risk (CSER) in Cambridge with philosopher Huw Price and Astronomer Royal Martin Rees — the origin story is a chance conversation between Price and Tallinn (famously, in a shared taxi) that convinced Price AI risk needed a Cambridge home. The same year, the Center for Applied Rationality launches in Berkeley. Its workshops train a large share of future safety researchers and anchor the community physically in Berkeley, where MIRI already sits and where CHAI, Redwood, MATS, and Constellation later cluster. A young researcher named Chris Olah receives a Thiel Fellowship.

In 2013, SIAI officially rebrands as the MIRI, while Sam Altman takes the helm at Y Combinator under Thiel's influence.

SCENE
2014 — The hinge year

The book: Bostrom publishes Superintelligence. Elon Musk, Bill Gates, and Stephen Hawking immediately begin quoting its dark warnings in public.

Musk
"We need to be super careful with AI. Potentially more dangerous than nukes."
Gates
"I am in the camp that is concerned about super intelligence."
Hawking
"Full artificial intelligence could spell the end of the human race."
all three quotes are real — Musk, X, Aug 2014 · Gates, Reddit AMA 2015 · Hawking, BBC 2014
IDEASSuperintelligence also hands the field its core arguments, still anchoring most safety agendas today: the orthogonality thesis (intelligence and goals are independent — a superintelligence can want anything, including paperclips), instrumental convergence (almost any goal implies grabbing resources and resisting shutdown), and the treacherous turn (a misaligned AI behaves — until it's strong enough not to).

The acquisition: Google buys DeepMind for over $500M. As a strict condition of sale, DeepMind demands an independent AI Ethics Board. Historical note: the board famously never met a single time.

The new guard: Max Tegmark, Jaan Tallinn, Viktoriya Krakovna, Anthony Aguirre, and Meia Chita-Tegmark found the Future of Life Institute (FLI). How they knew each other: Tegmark and Aguirre had run the Foundational Questions Institute together for a decade. Meia is Tegmark's wife. Krakovna was a Harvard statistics PhD active in Boston's rationalist community, and Tallinn arrived through the CSER network he had already co-founded. Vitalik Buterin joins the ecosystem as a Thiel Fellow.

Proof: Gates joins Musk & Hawking (Reddit AMA) · the acquisition & ethics-board condition
3

Labs start

2015–2021
safety people build the race
Jan 2015

Musk does not arrive cold: he is already an early DeepMind investor, has read Bostrom, and has spent months warning about AI in public. Tegmark invites him to FLI's closed-door Puerto Rico conference, the first time the academic existential-risk crowd and the industry people sit in one room. It produces an open research-priorities letter with thousands of signatures, and Musk pledges $10 million to FLI on the spot.

SCENE
Dec 2015 — OpenAI founded

Recognizing Google's grip on DeepMind talent, Sam Altman, Elon Musk, Greg Brockman, and Ilya Sutskever found OpenAI as a direct counterweight, with John Schulman, Andrej Karpathy, Wojciech Zaremba, and Durk Kingma on the founding research team. Backed by $1B in pledges from Musk, Thiel, Reid Hoffman, and Jessica Livingston, it is structured as a non-profit meant to share safety benefits freely.

How they met: Altman knew Brockman through Y Combinator, where Brockman was CTO of Stripe, one of its star companies; Musk and Altman were already friends. The plan came together over a group dinner at the Rosewood Hotel on Sand Hill Road in the summer of 2015. Brockman then recruited the research team, with Yoshua Bengio helping draw up the list of the strongest young researchers: Sutskever came from Google Brain, Schulman from Pieter Abbeel's Berkeley lab, and Karpathy, Zaremba, and Kingma from the same tight deep-learning circle spanning Google and academia.

Altman & Brockman
"…advance digital intelligence in the way that is most likely to benefit humanity, unconstrained by a need to generate financial return."
Musk
"With artificial intelligence we are summoning the demon."
real quotes — the founding post, Dec 11 2015 · Musk at MIT, Oct 2014
2016

Academia formalizes its involvement: Stuart Russell founds the Center for Human-Compatible AI (CHAI) at Berkeley using Open Philanthropy capital.

What comes out of CHAI: "assistance games," the formal agenda where an AI stays uncertain about human goals and defers to us; Russell's 2019 book Human Compatible; and a generation of alumni. Rohin Shah goes on to lead AGI-safety work at DeepMind, and Adam Gleave to found FAR AI.

SCENE
2017 — The strategic node link

Open Philanthropy officially spins out of GiveWell and injects a massive $30 million grant directly into OpenAI. Open Phil CEO Holden Karnofsky takes a seat on OpenAI's board.

The same year, Karnofsky marries Daniela Amodei — then a VP at OpenAI. Funding relationships and personal ones now run through the same small group of people; a yellow edge on the map marks it.

Karnofsky
Runs Open Phil. Just wired OpenAI $30M. Sits on its board.
Daniela Amodei
VP at OpenAI. Future co-founder and president of Anthropic.
Proof: Open Phil — OpenAI grant, 2017
2017

The technical spark. Chris Olah launches the technical publication Distill. Concurrently, Paul Christiano and OpenAI engineers publish the seminal paper on RLHF — the specific alignment technique that will later make conversational AI commercially viable.

IDEARLHF, in one breath: instead of hand-writing a goal, you train a reward model from human preference comparisons ("this answer is better than that one"), then optimize the AI against it. Born as an alignment technique; became the commercial engine of chatbots. Alongside it, interpretability — Olah's line of work — asks the complementary question: what is actually going on inside the network?
Proof: Christiano et al., 2017 (arXiv:1706.03741) · Distill
2018–19

In 2018, Elon Musk dramatically exits the OpenAI board following a failed internal control fight. Chris Olah joins OpenAI; Buck Shlegeris joins MIRI.

The original sin: in 2019, OpenAI makes a historic structural shift — a capped-profit arm and a $1 billion investment from Microsoft. The open-source non-profit ideal is abandoned; OpenAI stages a coordinated media campaign, refusing to release GPT-2 because it is "too dangerous for public consumption."

The countermoves: Open Philanthropy funds CSET with a historic ~$55M grant — its largest ever at the time — and grants MIRI $2.1M.

2020

In July, Connor Leahy, Sid Black, and Leo Gao form EleutherAI as an organic Discord server during pandemic lockdowns, to open-source models and bypass corporate gates. GPT-3 ships the same summer.

EleutherAI's role in this story is bigger than its size. Its GPT-Neo and GPT-J models and the Pile dataset prove that frontier-adjacent AI won't stay locked inside labs. The Discord also becomes a talent seedbed: Leahy and Black later found Conjecture, Gao later joins OpenAI's interpretability work, and much of the open-source interpretability community traces back to it.

The same year, Toby Ord publishes The Precipice — existential risk's most respectable mainstream treatment yet. And, disillusioned by OpenAI's commercial pivot under Microsoft, siblings Dario and Daniela Amodei quietly prepare to walk out.

SCENE
2021 — The great split

The core talent pool fractures across a dozen new vectors in an 18-month span.

The split: Dario and Daniela Amodei leave OpenAI with a core team of seven, including Chris Olah, Jared Kaplan, Tom Brown, Jack Clark, and Sam McCandlish, to found Anthropic. They all knew each other from inside OpenAI: Dario led the GPT-2/GPT-3 research organization, where Brown and McCandlish ran scaling, Olah ran interpretability, Clark ran policy, and Daniela, his sister, ran operations. Kaplan was an old physics friend Dario had pulled in as a consultant. Jaan Tallinn leads their $124 million Series A; Moskovitz joins.

The spin-offs: Paul Christiano leaves OpenAI to form the Alignment Research Center (ARC) — a small theory group working on aligning systems smarter than their overseers. Buck Shlegeris and the MIRI contingent launch Redwood Research — an applied-alignment nonprofit, now best known for "AI control": techniques for safely using models you don't fully trust. MATS launches (as SERI MATS) with Evan Hubinger — then a MIRI research fellow, later Anthropic's alignment stress-testing lead — mentoring the first scholars; Ryan Kidd and Christian Smith later scale it into an independent org. MATS scholars arrive mostly through the LessWrong/EA university pipeline, get mentored by researchers at Redwood, MIRI, and Anthropic, and alumni flow back into those same orgs, plus METR. And Vitalik Buterin donates millions in ETH (and reportedly SHIB) into FLI/MIRI reserves.

Dario Amodei
"…build more reliable, interpretable, and steerable AI systems."
Tallinn
Leads the $124M Series A. The Skype engineer who emailed Yudkowsky in 2009 now bankrolls the safety lab.
Dario's quote is from Anthropic's launch announcement
IDEASTwo ideas power this era's split. Scaling laws (Kaplan, McCandlish et al., 2020): model capability grows smoothly and predictably with compute and data — the discovery that turned safety debates into a race. And mesa-optimization / inner alignment (Hubinger et al., 2019): a trained model may develop its own internal goals that differ from the training objective — the formal version of "it might secretly want something else."
Proof: Anthropic $124M Series A, led by Tallinn · MATS · Scaling laws (arXiv:2001.08361) · Risks from Learned Optimization (arXiv:1906.01820)
4

It becomes big

2022–2023
money floods in, then craters
Early 2022

Sam Bankman-Fried is not new to this world: an MIT physics graduate and Jane Street trader, he was recruited into effective altruism's "earning to give" path by philosopher Will MacAskill, and built FTX explicitly to fund EA causes. Now his FTX Future Fund launches under Nick Beckstead, committing over $160 million to AI-safety initiatives within a few months.

Connor Leahy, Sid Black, and Gabriel Alfour use the boom to found Conjecture, a London startup that is the EleutherAI founders' change of heart. Having open-sourced large models, they now argue that was a mistake, and try to build bounded, understandable systems instead ("Cognitive Emulation"). It matters as the first venture-funded company whose product is safety itself. Investors include Karpathy, Nat Friedman, the Collison brothers, and SBF. Meanwhile Beth Barnes leaves OpenAI to establish ARC Evals, to test what models can actually do.

The same year, BlueDot Impact spins out of Cambridge's EA community, with Open Phil funding, to run AI Safety Fundamentals: the course, built on a curriculum by Richard Ngo, that becomes the field's main structured on-ramp for newcomers. It's also the host of the hackathon this very project was built for.

2021–23 — The ecosystem thickens

Around the labs, a professional layer grows. Allan Dafoe's Centre for the Governance of AI, born inside FHI, becomes an independent org (2021) and seeds the policy wing. Jaime Sevilla founds Epoch AI (2022), whose charts of compute and capability trends become the field's standard evidence base. Adam Gleave, the CHAI PhD, founds FAR AI in Berkeley (2022). Marius Hobbhahn co-founds Apollo Research in London (2023) to test frontier models for deception. And Constellation opens in Berkeley (2023), a shared hub that grew out of Redwood, where researchers from ARC, METR, MATS, and Open Phil work under one roof. Much of this layer is funded by Open Philanthropy and staffed by MATS alumni.

SCENE
Nov 30, 2022 — The retail wave

OpenAI drops ChatGPT. RLHF meets the general public, and the existential-risk debate instantly transforms from a theoretical subculture into a hyper-competitive tech race.

Altman
"today we launched ChatGPT. try talking with it here"
real post — Altman, X, Nov 30 2022 · launch page
IDEAA month later Anthropic publishes Constitutional AI: align a model against a short written constitution it critiques itself with, instead of thousands of human labels. The "safety lab" now has a signature technique to match OpenAI's RLHF. (arXiv:2212.08073)
Nov 2022 — The crater

The crypto exchange FTX collapses into financial fraud. The entire Future Fund team instantly resigns, the second-largest capital resource in the AI-safety ecosystem vaporizes overnight, and the public EA brand suffers catastrophic reputational damage. On the map, the green edges snap.

Proof: Bankruptcy of FTX, Nov 2022
Mar 2023

FLI issues an open letter demanding a 6-month pause on giant AI training runs. It garners 30,000+ signatures — including Elon Musk and Steve Wozniak — but development continues uninterrupted. Nobody pauses.

Proof: "Pause Giant AI Experiments" — full letter & signatures
SCENE
May 2023 — The consensus

The Center for AI Safety, founded the year before in San Francisco by Berkeley ML PhD Dan Hendrycks with Open Philanthropy funding, issues a single-sentence extinction statement:

"Mitigating the risk of extinction from AI should be a global priority alongside other societal-scale risks such as pandemics and nuclear war."
Geoffrey HintonYoshua BengioDemis HassabisSam AltmanDario Amodei+700 more

Signed by direct commercial rivals — Hassabis, Altman, Amodei — it is the ultimate "even the CEOs agree" moment. Geoffrey Hinton quits his post at Google the same period to speak freely about his existential fears.

Proof: full statement & signature list
Jul 2023

OpenAI's shield. In response to mounting pressure, OpenAI announces the Superalignment team, led by Ilya Sutskever and Jan Leike, promising 20% of the lab's total computing power to solve the core alignment problem within four years.

Proof: OpenAI — Introducing Superalignment
SCENE
Nov 2023 — The board crisis

The conflict between safety ideals and profit scaling reaches a breaking point inside OpenAI.

The coup: board members Helen Toner, Tasha McCauley, Adam D'Angelo, and Ilya Sutskever fire Sam Altman for a lack of candor.

The board
"…not consistently candid in his communications with the board."
Altman, 5 days later
"i love the openai team so much"
Shear
Interim CEO. For roughly 72 hours.
board statement and Altman post are real — OpenAI board statement, Nov 17 2023 · Altman, X

The snapback: within five days, investor pressure and a near-unanimous employee revolt force Altman back into power. The non-profit/EA-aligned board members are purged.

The state steps in: simultaneously, the UK hosts the Bletchley Park Summit, founding the UK AI Safety Institute (AISI) with Geoffrey Irving as chief scientist; the US AISI quickly follows. In December, ARC Evals spins out to become METR, with Beth Barnes as CEO.

IDEAEvals become the field's new currency: instead of grand theory about future superintelligence, orgs like METR and the AISIs test what today's models can actually do — deceive, self-replicate, assist with weapons — before release. METR's results become reference points for the whole field: the pre-release GPT-4 autonomy evaluations, and its 2025 "task-horizon" study finding that the length of tasks models can complete on their own doubles roughly every seven months. Safety work turns from philosophy into inspection.

In a single 30-day window, the safety movement loses its seat in the corporate boardroom and gains a permanent home inside state regulatory bodies.

Proof: the five days, documented · METR
5

The modern age

2024–2026
the diaspora, and consolidation
Feb–Apr 2024

Andrej Karpathy leaves OpenAI to found Eureka Labs. Holden Karnofsky steps down from his leadership role at Open Philanthropy.

The symbolic fall: after 19 years, the Future of Humanity Institute is officially closed by Oxford. The absolute founding institution of the field dies at the exact moment the broader AI-safety industry hits its peak.

SCENE
May 2024 — "Shiny products"

Ilya Sutskever and Jan Leike both abruptly quit OpenAI, days apart:

Sutskever
"After almost a decade, I have made the decision to leave OpenAI."
Leike
"…safety culture and processes have taken a backseat to shiny products."
real posts — Sutskever, X, May 14 2024 · Leike's full thread & coverage

OpenAI immediately dissolves the entire Superalignment team; the 20%-compute promise doesn't survive it. The realignments: Leike goes directly to Anthropic. Sutskever founds Safe Superintelligence (SSI), securing a $1 billion valuation on day one without a public product. Leopold Aschenbrenner publishes his explosive essay Situational Awareness and establishes an AGI-focused hedge fund.

SCENE
Aug 2024

John Schulman leaves OpenAI for Anthropic:

Schulman
"I've made the difficult decision to leave OpenAI… my desire to deepen my focus on AI alignment."
real post — Schulman, X, Aug 5 2024

The same month, Paul Christiano joins the US AISI (inside NIST) as Head of Safety — the lab-to-government pipeline in one arrow.

Oct 2024 — The ultimate validation

The global establishment canonizes the field's early pioneers: Demis Hassabis and John Jumper receive the Nobel Prize in Chemistry for AlphaFold, while Geoffrey Hinton receives the Nobel Prize in Physics. Durk Kingma joins Anthropic.

Proof: Nobel Chemistry 2024 · Nobel Physics 2024
2024 — The shift to advocacy

Recognizing that technical alignment cannot outpace commercial scaling speeds, MIRI officially pivots to pure political advocacy — abandoning its 20-year quest to solve alignment mathematically before models scale. Mira Murati leaves OpenAI to found Thinking Machines Lab.

SCENE
2025 — The loop closes

The boundary between safety funder and safety lab dissolves completely as Holden Karnofsky joins Anthropic as a full-time executive — closing the historic funder-to-operator loop. Yoshua Bengio establishes LawZero. John Schulman leaves Anthropic after ~6 months to become chief scientist at Thinking Machines Lab.

Karnofsky
Fifteen years deciding who gets funded. Now he works at the lab his foundation's world helped build — and his wife co-founded.
2025 — The label wars

The coalition that built this field is quietly splitting into camps. Post-FTX, "effective altruism" has become a brand many orgs avoid: CAIS positions itself as mainstream academic consensus-building and keeps its distance from EA framing; the AISIs are government bodies that can't afford the association; Anthropic is a commercial lab; and MIRI has gone full doom-advocacy.

The tension is real: the EA-descended wing thinks in decades and extinction probabilities; the newer professionalized wing thinks in evals, policy cycles, and quarterly capability jumps. Same family tree — increasingly different religions.

Apr 2025 — AI 2027

Daniel Kokotajlo — the OpenAI governance researcher who left in 2024 and refused to sign the exit NDA, giving up his equity to keep the right to criticize the company — publishes AI 2027 through his AI Futures Project, co-written with Scott Alexander of Astral Codex Ten. It is a detailed, month-by-month scenario of an intelligence explosion arriving in 2027, built on Kokotajlo's track record (his 2021 forecast "What 2026 Looks Like" aged unusually well). It reaches far beyond the field — the forecasting wing of the movement now has a mainstream audience.

Proof: AI 2027 — full scenario
Sep 2025 — The wilderness prophet

Eliezer Yudkowsky and Nate Soares publish If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies. The book becomes an instant New York Times bestseller — propelling a warning born in an obscure 1990s mailing list straight into mainstream retail culture, 25 years later. Same node the story opened on.

Nov 2025 — The corporate rebrand

Open Philanthropy rebrands its major operations as Coefficient Giving. Their own stated reasons: they had quietly become a multi-donor operation for years, and people kept confusing them with OpenAI and the Open Society Foundations. "Co" for the multi-donor collaboration, "efficient" for cost-effectiveness.

Proof: Coefficient Giving — their own explanation
SCENE
2026 — Consolidation

Connor Leahy steps down as CEO of Conjecture. Mustafa Suleyman fully consolidates consumer AI inside Microsoft. The UK and US AI Safety Institutes reach full operational capacity — staffed almost entirely by top-tier technical researchers defecting from the commercial labs they now help oversee.
