# AI Safety Network
# Live demo: https://nastassiashaveika.github.io/ai-safety-network/

This is a scrollytelling website that tells the story of the AI safety field from the 1990s extropians mailing list to the AI Safety Institutes of 2026. The map is inspired by the TV show Dark, a Russian oligarchy map, and a scrollytelling work done by Pudding. 

It has a narrative timeline that scrolls on the right; a force-directed network map of ~110 people, organizations, and funders stays pinned on the left and re-clusters as you scroll. Every name is clickable and opens a short backstory card (who they are, how they entered the field, what they do now). Edges are typed and color-coded: co-founding, employment, funding, and marriage. Key moments are staged as comic-style scenes with real, sourced quotes; recurring concepts (RLHF, scaling laws, mesa-optimization, evals, Constitutional AI) get plain-language idea cards; acronyms have hover tooltips.

Newcomers to AI safety face a wall of acronyms and org names with no story connecting them. Existing resources map who exists (the AI Safety Map at aisafety.com/map) but not how the field came to be: who funded whom, who left which lab to found what, how the same small network of people keeps reappearing. As I was job searching and engaging with resources, the same names would keep popping up, and a system appeared in my head – this resource is for people to spend less time figuring things out, a living document of the field!

Primary audience: AI Safety Fundamentals students, job seekers, and university-group members who need context before they can navigate orgs, agendas, and funders. 

Secondary: journalists and researchers who need the history and relationships quickly.

The path to impact is direct: it is a link you can send. Course facilitators can assign it as pre-reading, field-building orgs can embed it, and the underlying dataset (people, orgs, typed edges) can be extended by anyone.

# Accuracy and limits

All on-screen quotes are real, short, and linked to primary sources (original posts, announcements, papers). Paraphrases and dramatized panels are labeled as such. Contested or uncertain figures are flagged rather than stated as fact, and the site carries an explicit disclaimer: the opinions are my own; the research was done with the assistance of Claude, along with many conversations and online reading; and it remains a work in progress. It can be extended to other countries, more orgs and people, and simply have more pictures!
